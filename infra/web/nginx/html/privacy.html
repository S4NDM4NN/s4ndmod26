Privacy

Last updated: November 14, 2026. This describes what S4NDMoD26 actually stores and does today, and it will change as the project does.

Who we are

S4NDMoD26 is a hobby project that modernizes Return to Castle Wolfenstein multiplayer: browser and desktop play, player statistics, match history and replays. It is run from the United States. For anything below, write to s4ndm4nn@s4ndmod.com.

What is public

Statistics, match histories, replays and player pages are public, including for players with no account, shown under the name used in the game. If a key is linked to an account, its player page shows the account's username and picture and adds up all of that account's keys. Email addresses and the private key names are never public.

Linking keys to an account

You can link the keys your browser, the launcher and the original game client use to one account, so your games appear together. Linking is done by proving you hold the key. You can remove a link on your account page at any time.

Erasing your data

Email us, from the address on your account if you have one. We will check it is you (for a key, by asking you to sign a code with it). Then your games show "Deleted Player" instead of your name and key, your totals and player page are removed and your keys are unlinked. We can also delete your account. Other players' kills, deaths and results stay, since they are theirs too. Replays are not rewritten: they expire by themselves. Because the game servers' own statistics files still contain old lines, we keep a one-way hash of an erased key so the profile is not rebuilt from them.

You can delete your account yourself on the account page. That removes your username, email, sign-ins and links, but not your games: ask us to erase those too if you want.

Community servers

Independent servers may report statistics to this site in the future. Their operators keep their own recordings and logs under their own practices; this page covers what runs on s4ndmod.com.

Security

Passwords are hashed, sessions and tokens are stored hashed, and players prove their identity with signatures rather than passwords. No system is perfectly secure.

Your rights

Depending on where you live you may have the right to see, correct, export or delete personal data and to object to its use. Write to us and we will do what applies. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Changes

We update this page when what we do changes, and the date at the top says when.
